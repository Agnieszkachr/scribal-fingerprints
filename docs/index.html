The Typological Structure of Textual Variation

Agnieszka B. Ziemińska

A computational analysis of five Greek witnesses to the Gospel of John: P66, Codex Sinaiticus (ℵ), Codex Alexandrinus (A), Codex Vaticanus (B) and Codex Bezae (D). Every divergence from the baseline is annotated by type and embedded in its context, to ask whether the qualitative characterisations of these witnesses correspond to measurable, stable manuscript-level change profiles. This is the first stage of a longer project: the method is built and calibrated on one Gospel.

Key findings

Gospel of John, baseline B (Codex Vaticanus)

MeasureValueWhat it says
Annotated variants — Divergences of the four witnesses from B
Cramér’s V — Witness × variant type: a small association, distinguishable from its null
Cohen’s κ 0.88 The rule against a blind human reading, on the morphological/lexical distinction
Cohen’s d — Embedding separation: detectable, negligible
Adjusted Rand Index — Clustering recovers almost nothing of the witnesses (0 = chance)
Split-half gap — A witness’s own other half is closer than the halves of other witnesses
  • The typology is the robust result. The witnesses differ measurably in the kinds of change they introduce, under three witness baselines and the SBL edition, with directional patterns that match the qualitative descriptions in outline and correct them in detail.
  • The embeddings add little to it. The separation in embedding space is detectable but very weak, concentrated in Alexandrinus and all but absent within single variant classes; it is not a by-product of the witnesses’ different mixtures of variant types.
  • No sign that the separation is lexical. Hiding the content words of the context costs no more of the separation than hiding the same number of random words; the difference between the two is not distinguishable from zero.
All values are read from the pipeline output files. The analyses are repeated with Codex Vaticanus, Codex Alexandrinus and Codex Sinaiticus as baselines, and the typology also with the SBL edition. The profiles are read as properties of the manuscripts, which combine text-type, exemplar and scribe, not as fingerprints of individual scribes.
